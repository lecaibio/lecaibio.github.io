I work at the intersection of biology and ML. Stanford PhD in molecular biology with wet-lab depth in organ-on-a-chip and microbiome co-culture, now doing clinical ML on real patient-level data: TabPFN on EHR, synthetic data transfer learning. On the side, five years keeping production AWS infrastructure alive for a platform I built, serving tens of thousands of daily users. Doing both keeps me honest.

The bio+AI space has more depth gaps than hype gaps. Most people in it have one side, which is fine. I'm looking for the ones curious about the other side's story, and I speak enough of both languages to translate.

**Talk to me about:**

- checking a synthetic dataset before you train on it
- what you're pulling out of clinical free text, and where it breaks
- running ML infrastructure with no platform team
- whether your bio+AI idea has the data to support it

Or something else. That list is just what's on my desk lately, and half the conversations I've gotten the most out of started somewhere I wasn't expecting.

Coming into ML from biology? The whole sequence is in [Bio to ML: A Learning Path]({% post_url 2026-08-13-biology-to-ml-learning-path %}). It came out of questions from three summer interns I mentored. Read it, then email me about the block you got stuck on.

Email is best, and I answer. Coffee's on me if you're around the Bay, video if you're not. No proposal needed: tell me what you're working on and where it's stuck.
